Two pieces have joined since: an AI Search Watcher that checks whether ChatGPT and other AI search engines mention a brand, and a browser extension for quick metrics on whatever page you're already viewing. ## The thing all seven have in common: you're the trigger

None of Mangools' tools talk to each other on a schedule. KWFinder doesn't hand its winning keywords to SERPWatcher; SERPWatcher doesn't tell SiteProfiler a new page just started ranking. Each one waits in its own tab for a person to open it, ask it something, and come back later to check the answer - which is exactly the job it's built for, and exactly the seam DispatchSEO removes:

## A seat you renew every month vs a repo you already pay for

Mangools' own pricing copy puts its starting price at $37.70 a month across three tiers - Basic, Premium, and Agency - with extra seats billed on top and a free plan for occasional single lookups. That's a genuinely fair price for what it is: a shared login a team keeps paying for as long as anyone needs to open one of the seven tools.

DispatchSEO doesn't have a seat to renew, but it isn't free either - it needs a DataForSEO account of its own (billed to whoever runs the project, not bundled into a subscription tier) and a coding agent already running in a repo to drive the MCP server. If you're not already paying for an agent CLI for other work, standing one up just to dodge a $37.70 seat costs more setup time than it saves. If you are, the marginal cost of adding this project's research and tracking jobs to what that agent already does is close to zero - and it doesn't grow if a second or third person needs to see the results, the way an extra Mangools seat does.

## Where a login still beats a repo

Mangools stays the right tool when the job is genuinely exploratory - typing ten different seed keywords into KWFinder in twenty minutes to see what surfaces, or pulling up SiteProfiler on a competitor's domain mid-call. A person clicking through options and following a hunch is still faster than describing that hunch to an agent. It's also the simpler choice for a small team that wants a shared, point-and-click dashboard without anyone standing up a repo, an agent, or a DataForSEO account first - the free plan exists precisely for that occasional, no-commitment lookup.

Where the job is narrower and repeats - track a list of keywords, watch Search Console, keep a content pipeline moving without anyone remembering to open a tab - the automated half is the whole job, and a suite of switches to flip by hand starts costing more attention than it saves.

## FAQ

**What does Mangools actually include in 2026?**
Seven tools behind one login: KWFinder (keyword research), SERPChecker (SERP analysis), SERPWatcher (rank tracking), LinkMiner (backlink research), SiteProfiler (site metrics overview), an AI Search Watcher (AI-search brand visibility), and a browser extension. Most "alternative" articles still list only the first five.

**Does DispatchSEO replace Mangools' AI Search Watcher?**
For this project's own domain, yes - `get_ai_visibility` tracks citation rate across ChatGPT, Claude, and Google AI Overview on a weekly sweep. It isn't a general-purpose brand-monitoring tool for arbitrary domains the way Mangools' feature is; it only watches the site it's running for.

**Is DispatchSEO free the way Mangools has a free plan?**
No. It needs its own DataForSEO account (billed per call, to the project owner) and a coding agent subscription already running in a repo. There's no seat price, but there's no zero-cost tier either. For a single no-login check in the same spirit as Mangools' free tools, [the free keyword cannibalization checker](/free-tools/keyword-cannibalization-checker) runs one slice of this same pipeline in the browser.

**Can DispatchSEO do the kind of ad hoc keyword lookup KWFinder is built for?**
Not interactively in the same session. The `keyword_ideas` and `suggest_keywords` tools run as part of a scheduled research pass, not a type-and-see exploration loop - for open-ended brainstorming, KWFinder's UI is still the better fit.

**Do I need to already be running a coding agent to use this?**
Yes. DispatchSEO is MCP tools and crons an agent drives inside a repo - without one already in place, this isn't a lighter alternative to Mangools, it's a bigger project to take on first.

Mangools earns its price for what it is: seven well-made tools you decide when to open. The honest question isn't which one wins - it's whether picking the right tool and clicking into it is the workflow you want to keep doing by hand, or the part you'd rather a system did without being asked.
