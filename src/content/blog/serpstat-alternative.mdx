Serpstat packs 55 tools into one login - keyword research, a rank tracker, a site crawler, backlink and competitor analysis, [keyword clustering](/blog/best-keyword-clustering-tool), and an AI content assistant - priced from about $50/month on the annual Individual plan, verified against Serpstat's own pricing page. Every one of those tools ends the same way: a screen full of numbers and a person deciding what to do about them. DispatchSEO covers a narrower slice of that list - keyword research, rank tracking, and a project's own backlink signal - but skips the screen: the same kind of signal feeds a queue, a draft, and a shipped page without anyone opening a dashboard to make the call.

> **TL;DR** - Serpstat bundles six real jobs behind one login (keyword research, rank tracking, site audit, backlink/competitor analysis, keyword clustering, AI content tools), starting near $50/month billed annually with a 7-day trial. DispatchSEO covers three of the six as background automation instead of a dashboard, doesn't attempt a site crawl or a competitor-backlink index, and turns the gap it does see into a merged page on its own: this project shipped 7 guides this week, tracks 45 keywords including this page's own target, and runs on a 30-day-old account with a 0 DR-equivalent and 4 referring domains.

## Fifty-five tools behind one Serpstat login

Serpstat's own comparison page against Ahrefs puts a number on it directly: "55 tools for your Search Engine Optimization," priced under Ahrefs' cheapest tier at every point Serpstat checks - up to 10,000 tracked keywords against Ahrefs' 750 a week, 10,000 rows per report against 2,500, a 7-day free trial with no card required. The other three - a technical crawl, a competitor-backlink index, and thematic keyword clustering - aren't things this project's own account was built to do, and the gaps section further down says exactly why.

## Every module still hands the decision back to you

None of Serpstat's six tools act on their own output. The Rank Tracker logs a position drop; nothing inside Serpstat decides that the drop is worth a new page, drafts one, or ships it - that step waits for whoever opens the dashboard next, on whatever day they get to it. ## Where DispatchSEO doesn't even try to match Serpstat

A technical site crawl - broken links, missing metadata, duplicate content, the page-by-page sweep Serpstat's Site Audit runs - needs a purpose-built crawler walking every URL on a site, and nothing here does that; Search Console surfaces some of the same symptoms as a side effect of already being connected, which is incidental reporting, not a scheduled audit. Competitor-backlink research is the same story from the link side: `get_domain_rank` and `get_backlink_prospects` read this project's own connected account, not a crawl-scale index of the open web the way Serpstat's Backlink Analysis does - [the Majestic alternative guide](/blog/majestic-alternative) covers that same honest ceiling from the pure-index side. Keyword clustering exists on this site, just not inside the backend: [a free clustering tool](/free-tools/keyword-clustering-tool) runs the browser-side version of that specific job, no login and no queue involved.

## Fifty-five tools are the right call for some of this

Stay on Serpstat (or move to a similarly broad suite) when several of those six modules genuinely need to be open at once - an agency running technical audits and backlink research across a dozen client domains, or a team that wants one shared login nobody has to wire up a repo or an agent CLI to use. It's also the simpler pick for exploratory work: typing keyword after keyword into a research tool to see what surfaces is still faster by hand than describing that hunch to an agent. One site, one content queue that keeps growing regardless of who's watching it - that's the shape [this project's own SERP-tracking writeup](/blog/serp-tracking-api) covers from the infrastructure side, and it's a smaller shape than a 55-tool suite is built to fill.

## FAQ

**How many tools does Serpstat actually include?**
Serpstat's own materials put the count at 55, spanning six broader jobs: keyword research, rank tracking, site audit, backlink and competitor analysis, keyword clustering, and AI-assisted content tools.

**What does Serpstat cost?**
The annual Individual plan runs $602/year (about $50/month), verified against Serpstat's own vs-Ahrefs pricing page, with a 7-day free trial and no card required to start it.

**Does DispatchSEO replace Serpstat's Site Audit?**
No. Nothing here crawls a site's pages for technical or on-page issues. Search Console surfaces indexing problems as a side effect of being connected, which isn't the same job as a scheduled crawl.

**Does DispatchSEO replace Serpstat's backlink and competitor analysis?**
Only for a project's own connected DataForSEO account, and only at that account's scale - not a crawl-scale index of the open web the way Serpstat's Backlink Analysis module works.

**Is there a free way to try keyword clustering without Serpstat or DispatchSEO?**
[The free keyword clustering tool](/free-tools/keyword-clustering-tool) on this site runs that specific job in the browser, no login required - it's a separate surface from the backend queue this guide otherwise describes.

**Do I need an agent CLI already running to get value from DispatchSEO?**
Yes. It's MCP tools and crons an agent drives inside a repo - without one already doing other work, standing one up just to avoid a Serpstat seat is a bigger project than the seat itself.

Fifty-five tools earn their price when several of them genuinely need to be open in the same afternoon - a crawl, a competitor index, a content brief. Most people typing "Serpstat alternative" are sitting on something smaller than that: one site, one queue, and whether the next page waits for someone to open a dashboard or gets decided the moment the data says it's ready.
