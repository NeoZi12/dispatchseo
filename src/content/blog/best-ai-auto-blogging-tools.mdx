For 30 WordPress posts a month, pick Outrank or SEObot. For a blog that lives in a GitHub repo, pick RightBlogger or DispatchSEO. To see finished articles before paying anything, DispatchSEO's free plan is the only one of the six that publishes them. We make DispatchSEO, and every price below was read from the vendor's own pricing page on 2026-11-09.

> **TL;DR** - Outrank ($83 to $99 a month) and RightBlogger ($49 to $59) are the volume tools at about 30 articles a month. Autoblogging.ai is the cheapest paid entry at $19 for about ten articles, and SEObot starts at $49 and publishes to the most platforms. DispatchSEO publishes two fact-checked articles a month free and one to five a week for $19 to $69, to a GitHub repo or a hosted blog.

RightBlogger's and Autoblogging.ai's headline prices above are their yearly rates, so DispatchSEO's yearly rate is shown too.

## Which ones check facts before publishing?

SEObot's homepage claims fact-checking with cited sources. Autoblogging.ai lists a fact checker on all paid plans that verifies claims against live sources. Outrank mentions fact-checking only on its $1,399 a month Human Curated tier, where editors review each piece. DispatchSEO checks every claim in a draft against the passage of the source it cites and parks an article that cannot be grounded in more than one source; [how we check](/blog/content-freshness-seo) describes the gate. RightBlogger's and Arvow's pricing pages do not mention fact-checking, which is not the same as saying they have none.

## Which ones publish on autopilot, and which let you look first?

SEObot calls itself 100% autopilot by default and offers an approve-or-decline email. Outrank's base plan publishes automatically. Arvow describes automatic publishing and updating with no approval step. Autoblogging.ai lets you review before publishing or auto-post. RightBlogger keeps posts as drafts or, on Git, opens a pull request you merge. DispatchSEO publishes the moment an article passes its gate; on a GitHub site that is a pull request that merges itself when the site is on Auto, or waits for you when it is not.

## What about sites built from code?

Of the six we checked, two document publishing into a Git repository. RightBlogger's Git integration commits the post and its images and opens a pull request by default, with a toggle to commit straight to the production branch. DispatchSEO does the same from its server: it reads a post already in your repo, mirrors its frontmatter, commits on a branch, opens the pull request and merges it when the site is on Auto. SEObot lists Next.js among its targets, but its page does not say how. If your site has no repo, DispatchSEO hosts the blog instead; RightBlogger does not.

## Where does each tool win?

RightBlogger is the broadest product: a writing toolkit, keyword research, a post-publish Site Agent that audits older posts, and Git publishing. It suits a solo blogger who wants one subscription for everything. SEObot suits someone who wants 20 posts a month to appear on a Framer or Notion site without opening a dashboard. Outrank gives the most articles per dollar at the 30-a-month mark and is the only one with a human-edited tier. Autoblogging.ai has the lowest paid entry and also posts to Medium, Dev.to and Hashnode. Arvow has the most articles per dollar of all, for people who will do their own checking. DispatchSEO suits someone who wants to see output before paying, whose site lives in a repo, or who would rather publish one to five checked articles a week on topics with measured demand than thirty on guessed ones; it has no writing assistant and does not support Wix, Squarespace, Shopify or Webflow.

## How do I choose?

Two questions. Where does your blog live, and how many articles a month do you want? WordPress and 30 a month: Outrank or SEObot. WordPress and a few a month: Autoblogging.ai. A Git repo: [RightBlogger or DispatchSEO](/blog/dispatchseo-vs-rightblogger). Not sure yet: the [free plans that publish anything](/blog/free-auto-blogging-tools) are a short list.

## FAQ

**Is auto-blogging safe for Google rankings?** Google's spam policy targets pages produced in bulk to manipulate rankings, however they were made. Thirty unchecked articles a month on guessed topics carry that risk; a few checked articles on topics with real demand carry much less. Volume is the risk, not the AI.

**Which of these is open source?** DispatchSEO (AGPL-3.0), which can be self-hosted. None of the others we checked list public source code.

**What does one article cost on each tool?** From about 60 cents to about $4.40, depending on how many the plan includes and whether anything checks them. [What an AI-written SEO article costs in 2026](/blog/what-an-ai-seo-article-costs) works it out per tool.
