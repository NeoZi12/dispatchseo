Your SEO tool budget is mostly hours, not dollars. Google Search Console plus a free keyword source covers what a one-site business needs, and the paid plans start at $29 a month and climb to $139 or more. What decides whether SEO happens is whether anyone has the four or five weekly hours it takes to use any of them.

> **TL;DR** - Start at $0: Search Console, Keyword Planner and Ahrefs Free. A paid plan only earns its price once you track more than a handful of keywords or watch competitors. The expensive part of the stack is your own time, so the choice that matters is who does the weekly work, not which dashboard shows it.

## Which free tools carry the core work?

Three free tools cover the jobs a single site has:

- **Google Search Console** reports indexing, the queries you appear for, clicks and average position. Google's own starter guide points to it for monitoring and checking individual pages with the URL Inspection tool, and it never says you need to pay for a tool.
- **Google Keyword Planner** gives volume ranges for topic ideas. It needs a Google Ads account, which you can open without running ads.
- **Ahrefs Free** lets you see Ahrefs data for a site you verify, so you get a crawl-based audit without a card.

## At what point does a paid plan pay for itself?

A paid plan earns its fee when it does something the free tier can't, and you'll use that thing weekly. Three honest triggers:

1. You track more keywords than Search Console's query list makes practical, or you need daily checks on a fixed set.
2. You compete against a named handful of sites and need their backlinks and top pages.
3. You run more than one site, so the per-project pricing finally gets used.

If none of those is true, the $29 tier is the most you should consider, and waiting is free. If one is true, the $129 to $139 range is sized for it, but read the project and keyword limits before assuming it fits.

## Which tools can a small site leave out?

Most of the long listicle can wait:

| Skip for now | Why it can wait |
|---|---|
| All-in-one suites | Built for several sites and teams |
| Content scorers | They grade pages you haven't published yet |
| Backlink checkers | Outreach comes after you have pages worth linking to |
| Paid rank trackers | Search Console already reports average position |
| Browser SEO toolbars | Numbers with nothing to act on yet |

A local business has one extra free item that isn't a "tool" at all: a complete Google Business Profile. It matters more for map results than any dashboard does.

## When does automation beat a tool stack?

When the missing ingredient is the weekly work rather than the data. [The SEO automation tools guide](/blog/best-seo-automation-tools) sorts what exists by the job it automates: rank trackers watch, AI writers draft, workflow platforms connect. DispatchSEO is the version that does the loop: it tracks Search Console and ranks on a schedule and queues ideas for your coding agent to build as pull requests, so your part is approving and merging. It fits one site run by an owner who would rather review than research.

## When is a bigger stack the right call?

If you run client sites, you need per-client reports and competitor data, and the mid-range plans are built for that. If your site is past a few hundred pages, a crawler beyond the free tier will find problems Search Console won't. And if you like doing the research yourself, do it - the time argument only bites when the work isn't getting done.

## FAQ

**What are the best SEO tools for a small business on a budget?**
Google Search Console, Google Keyword Planner and Ahrefs Free. They cost nothing and cover indexing, topic ideas and rank for a single site.

**How much do SEO tools cost for a small business?**
From $0 to roughly $139 a month for a paid plan. Ahrefs lists Starter at $29 and Lite at $129, and Semrush lists its SEO plan at $139 a month on monthly billing.

**Do I need Semrush or Ahrefs as a small business?**
Not at the start. Both are sized for several sites, and a single site gets more from Search Console until it needs competitor data.

**Is free SEO software good enough?**
For one site, yes. The limit isn't the data but the weekly time to act on it.

**Can I automate SEO for my small business?**
Yes. Once you've seen the manual loop once, a manager that researches, builds pages for review and tracks results turns your job into approving.
