Two free plans publish an article to your site: DispatchSEO (two fact-checked articles a month, with a small badge, for sites that live in a GitHub repo) and Autoblogging.ai (10 credits a month, enough for a few plain articles through your own WordPress connection). RightBlogger's free tier writes 2,000 words but does not publish, Outrank's trial articles are watermarked, and SEObot and Arvow have no free plan. We make DispatchSEO, and each row below quotes the vendor's own page as of 2026-11-09.

> **TL;DR** - "Free auto-blogging" usually means free words, a watermarked trial, or a free plan that researches, writes and publishes. Only DispatchSEO and Autoblogging.ai do the third. DispatchSEO's two articles arrive finished, as a pull request to your GitHub repo, with no card and no trial clock; Autoblogging.ai's ten credits cover about two full articles or ten plain ones through your own WordPress connection.

## What should "publishes for you" mean?

A free plan that hands you 2,000 words is a free writing assistant, which is useful but is not auto-blogging. The test for this page: on the free plan, with no card, does an article end up live on a site you own without you pasting anything? DispatchSEO and Autoblogging.ai pass, Autoblogging.ai with a caveat: ten credits is ten plain articles or two or three with images, analysis and links, and you connect the destination yourself. DispatchSEO's two articles arrive complete, with a cover image, internal links and the citations checked.

## What is the catch on each free plan?

RightBlogger's free tier is the editor without the automation, so you see the writing but not the autopilot. Autoblogging.ai's ten credits are a sample of its credit system. DispatchSEO's free plan is the product at a slower pace, and every free article carries a small "published with DispatchSEO" badge; the paid plans remove it. There is no trial clock that converts you to a paid plan. A free site pauses only after 60 days without a sign-in, with one warning email first, and resumes when you come back.

## When is free enough?

Two articles a month is a real cadence for a side project or a new SaaS in its first quarter, when the job is a dozen indexed pages and a Search Console you actually read; [SEO for SaaS](/blog/seo-for-saas) lays out that first year. It is not a cadence for a site competing on volume. If you need a post a day, no free plan gets you there; the cheapest paid routes are RightBlogger Solo at $49 billed yearly and Arvow at $39 if its yearly rate holds, both covered in [best AI auto-blogging tools](/blog/best-ai-auto-blogging-tools).

A free plan is also the only way to judge quality on your own topics. Sample articles on a vendor's site were written on topics that show the tool off; the free plan writes about yours.

## FAQ

**Does the free DispatchSEO plan need a GitHub repo?** Yes. DispatchSEO works only with websites that live in a GitHub repo (Next.js, Astro, Hugo, anything you deploy from code), and each article arrives as a pull request. WordPress, Shopify, Wix, Squarespace, Webflow and Ghost are not supported. Support for Lovable sites is coming soon.

**Will two free articles a month hurt my site?** Google's spam rules target bulk pages made to manipulate rankings. Two fact-checked articles a month on topics people search for is the opposite of bulk.

**Can I remove the badge without paying?** No. The badge is the price of the free plan. Starter at $19 a month removes it and raises the pace to 5 articles per month.
