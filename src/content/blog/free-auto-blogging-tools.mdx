"Free auto-blogging tool" usually means one of three things: a free plan that writes words you still have to publish yourself, a watermarked trial, or a free plan that actually researches, writes and publishes an article to your site. Of the six tools checked on 2026-11-09, only two do the third thing, and one of them is ours. Here is what each free tier really includes, read from the vendors' own pricing pages, so you can pick without signing up for six trials.

> **TL;DR** - DispatchSEO's free plan publishes two fact-checked articles a month to your GitHub repo or a hosted blog, with no card and no end date, in exchange for a small badge on free articles. Autoblogging.ai gives 10 credits a month, enough for a handful of plain articles you can push to WordPress. RightBlogger's free tier is 2,000 AI words a month with no automation. Outrank shows watermarked trial articles. SEObot and Arvow have no free plan. If you want a published article for $0, the list is two tools long.

## What "publishes for you" has to mean

A free plan that hands you 2,000 words is a free writing assistant, which is useful but is not auto-blogging. The test for this page was simple: on the free plan, with no card, does an article end up live on a site you own without you pasting anything? DispatchSEO and Autoblogging.ai pass. Autoblogging.ai passes with a caveat: ten credits is ten plain articles or about two with images, analysis and links, and you connect the destination yourself. DispatchSEO's two articles arrive complete, with a cover image, internal links and the citations checked, as a pull request to your repo or on a blog it hosts.

## What the free plan is for, from each vendor's side

Vendors do not give articles away for nothing, so it is worth knowing the deal. RightBlogger's free tier is a taste of the editor, meant to sell the $49 suite. Autoblogging.ai's ten credits are a sample of the credit system. DispatchSEO's free plan is the product at a slower pace: every free article carries a small "published with DispatchSEO" badge, which is how the free tier pays for itself, and the paid plans remove it. There is no trial clock; a site can stay on two a month indefinitely.

## When free is enough, and when it is not

Two articles a month is a real cadence for a side project or a new SaaS in its first quarter, when the job is to get a dozen indexed pages and read Search Console. It is not enough for a site competing on volume. If you need a post a day, no free plan gets you there; the cheapest route is Autoblogging.ai's $49 Regular plan or Outrank at $99.

A free plan is also the only way to judge quality on your own topics. Sample articles on a vendor's site were written on the topics that show the tool off. The free plan writes about yours.

## Frequently asked

**Does the free DispatchSEO plan need a GitHub repo?** No. If your site has a repo, the article arrives as a pull request. If it does not, DispatchSEO hosts the blog. It does not yet support Wix, Squarespace, Shopify or Webflow sites.

**Will two free articles a month hurt my site?** Google's spam rules target bulk pages made to manipulate rankings. Two fact-checked articles a month on topics people search for is the opposite of bulk.

**Can I remove the badge without paying?** No. The badge is the price of the free plan. Starter at $19 a month removes it and raises the pace to one article a week.
