> **TL;DR:** SaaS keyword research works when you start from the buyer's own words and rank keywords by buying intent before volume. In a 2023 Grow and Convert case study for Geekbot, bottom-of-funnel articles converted at 4.78% against 0.19% for top-of-funnel ones, so treat the volume number as one input, not the verdict ([growandconvert.com](https://www.growandconvert.com/content-marketing/saas-keyword-research/)).

| Step | What you do | What you should see |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Define the buyer | Write two sentences: who they are, why your product fits | Both sentences written without vagueness |
| 2. Collect their phrasing | Pull verbatim phrases from calls, tickets, reviews, threads | A phrase bank in the buyer's words |
| 3. Brainstorm three types | Category, competitors and alternatives, jobs to be done | A seed sheet with three buckets |
| 4. Verify intent | Read the SERP for each candidate | Pages that sell or compare, not only explain |
| 5. Check volume and KD | Tune both together in a tool | Keywords that are reachable and still worth it |
| 6. Map and re-run | Match each keyword to a page, repeat every three to six months | One target page per keyword |
| **Verdict** | Geekbot: BOTF 4.78% vs TOTF 0.19% conversion | **Rank by intent first** |

## Who is the keyword for? Write the buyer definition first

**Prerequisites:** a live product (or a clear draft of one), a blank spreadsheet, and an afternoon. Free tools are enough; one source states the process can be followed with free alternatives to Ahrefs ([madx.digital](https://www.madx.digital/resource/keyword-research-guide)).

1. **Write two sentences.** The first covers segment, company size, role and the situation the buyer is in right now. The second says why your product is the obvious fit for that person. **You should see:** two sentences a stranger could repeat back. If you cannot write both, stop here, because keyword research will only make the vagueness look organised ([platypusseo.com](https://platypusseo.com/blog/saas-keyword-research/)).
2. **Optional: split by role.** If you already have customers, segment them by company size, industry, job title and common pain points ([linkedin.com](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/how-conduct-b2b-saas-specific-keyword-research-better-james-gunn-rcvhc)). Then map keywords to the people on the buying committee (C-suite, IT, department heads or end-users) and their concerns. Gartner reports that 64% of B2B purchase decisions involve four or more people, so one persona rarely covers the whole sale. **You should see:** a short list of roles, each with one concern.

If you are a solo founder with a handful of customers, skip step 2 and revisit it once you have more data.

## Where do you find the words buyers actually use?

Build a phrase bank. Keep every phrase verbatim; do not tidy it into marketing language.

- [ ] Sales calls and demo recordings
- [ ] Support tickets
- [ ] G2 and Capterra reviews, for your product and competitors ([platypusseo.com](https://platypusseo.com/blog/saas-keyword-research/))
- [ ] Community threads
- [ ] Your site's internal search queries, which show what visitors want once they arrive and flag what your content is missing ([questiondb.io](https://questiondb.io/hub/saas-keyword-research/))
- [ ] Prompts buyers type into ChatGPT, alongside what they type into Google ([platypusseo.com](https://platypusseo.com/blog/saas-keyword-research/))

The last item matters more each quarter. Peec's analysis of 20 million ChatGPT fan-out queries found average length roughly doubled from about six words to about twelve between October 2025 and January 2026 ([platypusseo.com](https://platypusseo.com/blog/saas-keyword-research/)). A G2 survey found 22% of buyers' first chatbot prompts are requirements-based: the situation first, the category or competitor second. So save full sentences that describe a situation, not just two-word terms.

Then get marketing, sales and support in one room. They talk to customers directly and will surface ideas a tool never will ([questiondb.io](https://questiondb.io/hub/saas-keyword-research/)). If you ask ChatGPT for seed ideas, label them unvalidated, since none of it is backed by real search data yet.

**You should see:** a sheet of raw phrases, none yet checked for volume.

## Which three keyword types should you brainstorm?

Grow and Convert's framework names three types of buying-intent keywords: category keywords, competitors and alternatives, and jobs to be done.

| Bucket | Example shape | Pitfall |
|---|---|---|
| **Category** | "CRM software for manufacturing" instead of "CRM software" ([linkedin.com](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/how-conduct-b2b-saas-specific-keyword-research-better-james-gunn-rcvhc)) | The super obvious terms are often highly competitive ([growandconvert.com](https://www.growandconvert.com/content-marketing/saas-keyword-research/)) |
| **Competitors and alternatives** | "[competitor] alternatives", "[you] vs [competitor]" | A competitor targeting a term does not make it relevant to you ([growandconvert.com](https://www.growandconvert.com/content-marketing/saas-keyword-research/)) |
| **Jobs to be done** | Question-style phrases from People Also Ask or AnswerThePublic | Easy to drift into purely informational topics |

**Category.** If you can go beyond the first half dozen terms that describe your product, you can probably find valuable keywords with lower competition ([growandconvert.com](https://www.growandconvert.com/content-marketing/saas-keyword-research/)). Industry modifiers help: Ahrefs states that keywords with industry modifiers have lower competition but higher relevance ([linkedin.com](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/how-conduct-b2b-saas-specific-keyword-research-better-james-gunn-rcvhc)). Find modifiers in Google Keyword Planner or Ahrefs' Keyword Explorer, and check which ones competitors' product pages use successfully.

**Competitors and alternatives.** Six competitor comparison articles for Grow and Convert's client Circuit brought in 149 organic signups over two years ([growandconvert.com](https://www.growandconvert.com/content-marketing/saas-keyword-research/)). Small page count, real signups.

**Jobs to be done.** Click into a few People Also Ask questions and more related questions keep surfacing ([questiondb.io](https://questiondb.io/hub/saas-keyword-research/)). AnswerThePublic is another source for long-tail questions about your product's features ([linkedin.com](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/how-conduct-b2b-saas-specific-keyword-research-better-james-gunn-rcvhc)).

**You should see:** a seed sheet with each phrase tagged by bucket, and at least a few modifier variants of your main category term.

## Which keywords actually earn signups: intent or volume?

Here is the worked example. For the Geekbot client, Grow and Convert compared articles aimed at bottom-of-funnel (BOTF) keywords with those aimed at top-of-funnel (TOTF) ones ([growandconvert.com](https://www.growandconvert.com/content-marketing/saas-keyword-research/)).

| | BOTF articles | TOTF articles |
|---|---|---|
| Articles published | 22 | 42 |
| Organic traffic | 28,190 pageviews | 204,303 visitors |
| Conversion rate | 4.78% | 0.19% |
| Conversions | 1,348 | 397 |

The TOTF set had nearly twice as many articles and nearly 10X the organic traffic, yet produced fewer conversions ([growandconvert.com](https://www.growandconvert.com/content-marketing/saas-keyword-research/)). The reason is simple: very few readers attracted by top-of-funnel content will ever be in the market for your product.

**Decision rule:** skip generic high-volume terms that pull in unqualified traffic. Long-tail keywords with lower volume but real conversion intent tend to win, because the searchers already know roughly what they want ([questiondb.io](https://questiondb.io/hub/saas-keyword-research/)).

**When this is the wrong call:** if your goal is brand awareness or building an email list rather than signups, top-of-funnel terms have a job. For a founder who needs paying users, they come second.

## How do you verify buying intent before you write anything?

Checking the SERP is the second step in Grow and Convert's process. Do it for every candidate on your sheet.

| Step | What to do | What you should see |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Search the term in a private window, set to your target country | Results a typical buyer there would get |
| 2 | Read the first page | Product pages, comparison pages, "best X for Y" lists, alternatives pages |
| 3 | Look for an AI Overview | Present or absent, noted in a column |
| 4 | Label the row | **buy**, **compare** or **learn** |

If the first page is mostly definitions and how-tos, the term is informational. Label it **learn** and move it down.

### Reading the AI Overview signal

On Google, Seer Interactive's study of 2.43 billion impressions saw an AI Overview for 95.4% of comparison-format pages ([platypusseo.com](https://platypusseo.com/blog/saas-keyword-research/)). Transactional queries are much less exposed. Seer saw them on 4.9% of those queries, and an experiment by Agarwal and Sen saw them on 6% of transactional searches against 53% of informational ones. The cost of showing up is real: across 300,000 keywords, Ahrefs put the click loss at 58% of position-one clicks wherever an AI Overview appears.

So a **learn** term with an AI Overview is the weakest row on your sheet. A **buy** term without one is the strongest.

### Spread the sheet across stages

Do not let every row sit at the same level of buyer certainty ([platypusseo.com](https://platypusseo.com/blog/saas-keyword-research/)). Keep a mix of problem-aware terms, comparison terms and ready-to-buy terms, and fill the buy rows first.

### Our position on comparison pages

Write them anyway. Comparison pages are where a buyer actually chooses, so judge them by signups rather than clicks. Semrush found that almost 90% of the time, the pages ChatGPT cites sit at organic position 21 or lower, and that AI-referred visitors convert at around four times the organic rate ([platypusseo.com](https://platypusseo.com/blog/saas-keyword-research/)). Ranking first is not the only route to being seen.

**You should see:** every row on your sheet marked buy, compare or learn, with an AI Overview flag beside it.

## How do you mine competitors without copying them?

1. **Pick three to five competitors**, the ones that actually show up in your SERP checks ([linkedin.com](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/how-conduct-b2b-saas-specific-keyword-research-better-james-gunn-rcvhc)).
2. **Run a gap tool.** In Semrush's Keyword Gap, enter your domain plus up to four competitor domains, confirm your target country, and click Compare ([questiondb.io](https://questiondb.io/hub/saas-keyword-research/)). The tool finds keywords they rank for that you don't ([linkedin.com](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/how-conduct-b2b-saas-specific-keyword-research-better-james-gunn-rcvhc)).
3. **Select Missing** in the keyword table to see the gaps ([questiondb.io](https://questiondb.io/hub/saas-keyword-research/)).
4. **Filter for relevance.** Just because a competitor targets a keyword does not make it relevant. If small businesses aren't your market, don't target "X software for small businesses" ([growandconvert.com](https://www.growandconvert.com/content-marketing/saas-keyword-research/)).
5. **Do not assume rankings equal revenue.** Lots of first-page rankings do not necessarily mean those articles bring in conversions ([growandconvert.com](https://www.growandconvert.com/content-marketing/saas-keyword-research/)).
6. **Mix difficulty.** Target the competitive keywords right away, since ranking against big names takes longer. For faster results, add a few less competitive keywords with high buying intent, but don't ignore the competitive ones ([growandconvert.com](https://www.growandconvert.com/content-marketing/saas-keyword-research/)).

That last point answers whether a small site can compete with big names: yes, on the terms they ignore, and over time on the contested ones, as long as you start the contested ones now.

**You should see:** a trimmed gap list where every row passes the "is this my buyer?" test.

## How do you read volume and KD without trusting them blindly?

Treat volume and KD as a pair. Adjust both until the keywords are within reach and still worth the effort ([questiondb.io](https://questiondb.io/hub/saas-keyword-research/)). There is no universal cutoff, so set yours by what your own site can plausibly rank for.

| Signal | Keep or cut? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| High intent, high volume | Keep, write first | The best case |
| High intent, low volume | Keep, write next | Grow and Convert prefers it over low intent with high volume once the best case runs out ([growandconvert.com](https://www.growandconvert.com/content-marketing/saas-keyword-research/)) |
| Low intent, high volume | Cut or move down | Pulls in readers unlikely to buy |
| Zero volume in the tool, but the phrase came from a customer call and the SERP shows buying intent | Keep | See below |

### Why zero volume is not a veto

The most specific B2B phrases often show "zero volume" in Ahrefs or Semrush ([platypusseo.com](https://platypusseo.com/blog/saas-keyword-research/)). Let the tool inform the call instead of making it. If a buyer used the exact wording on a call and the SERP looks like a buying page, keep the row.

Long-tail terms also match how buyers search. A Conductor study found that 48% of B2B buyers use long-tail keywords during research ([linkedin.com](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/how-conduct-b2b-saas-specific-keyword-research-better-james-gunn-rcvhc)).

### A simple score

Give each term a search priority score ([madx.digital](https://www.madx.digital/resource/keyword-research-guide)). Make it a plain one: intent label first, reachable KD second, volume only as a tiebreaker. Paid suites are optional here. Our roundup of [the best free SEO tools](/blog/best-free-seo-tools) shows what the free options do well and where they leave gaps.

**You should see:** a ranked list where the top rows are high intent, reachable, and phrased the way buyers talk.

## What do you do with the list, and when is this process the wrong choice?

### Map and schedule

Match each target keyword to a page you already have, or flag it as a new page ([madx.digital](https://www.madx.digital/resource/keyword-research-guide)). Poor targets burn time and budget. Review the list every three to six months, or sooner if customers or the market visibly change ([questiondb.io](https://questiondb.io/hub/saas-keyword-research/)).

| Row on your sheet | Action |
|---|---|
| Matches an existing page | Update that page |
| No matching page | Add to the new-page queue |
| Labeled learn, no AI Overview | Schedule after the buy and compare rows |

### When this is the wrong process

- **You cannot write the buyer definition.** Fix positioning first, because keyword research on a vague audience only produces a tidy-looking list of the wrong terms.
- **Your buyers research mainly in chatbots.** G2's March 2026 survey of 1,076 B2B software buyers reported that 71% use AI chatbots to research software ([platypusseo.com](https://platypusseo.com/blog/saas-keyword-research/)). On Ahrefs' own site, AI search accounted for 0.5% of visits yet 12.1% of signups. If that looks like your audience, add prompt-level tracking to the keyword work instead of replacing it.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I do SaaS keyword research without paying for Ahrefs or Semrush?**

Yes. One source states you can follow its process using free alternatives to Ahrefs [3]. Google Keyword Planner can surface industry modifiers [4], and People Also Ask is free to click through [5]. The free route costs you speed, not method, so lean harder on your own customer phrases.

**How many competitors should I include in a keyword gap analysis?**

Start with your top three to five competitors [4]. The Semrush Keyword Gap tool takes your domain plus up to four competitors [5], so a set of five fits in one run. Add more only if the first run gives you too few relevant gaps.

**Do I still need keyword research if my buyers ask ChatGPT instead of Google?**

Yes, because the work is now about phrasing as much as ranking. In the G2 survey, 69% of buyers said a chatbot led them to a different vendor than they had planned [2]. A 2026 Wynter survey of 101 mid-market SaaS CMOs found 84% now use AI tools for vendor discovery, up from 24% a year earlier [2]. Your phrase bank of buyer situations is what lets you write pages those tools can cite.
