RightBlogger and DispatchSEO are the only two auto-blogging tools that will commit an article to your GitHub repository and open a pull request. Past that, they are built for different people. RightBlogger is a writing suite with automation bolted on: unlimited AI words, dozens of writing tools, keyword research, and a Site Agent that audits your old posts, from $49 a month billed yearly. DispatchSEO is a narrower autopilot: it picks topics with measured search demand, writes and fact-checks one article at a time, publishes it, and shows you the Google clicks it brought, free for two articles a month and $19 to $69 for one to five a week. We make DispatchSEO, so this page tries to be fair to RightBlogger by quoting its own pages.

> **TL;DR** - Pick RightBlogger if you write some of your own posts, want 30 automated posts a month on one site, or publish to Webflow, Ghost or Duda. Pick DispatchSEO if you want to see real published articles before paying, your site has no repo and needs a hosted blog, you care more about a fact check than about volume, or you want the Google stats per article in the same place. Both open pull requests; only DispatchSEO merges them for you when the site is on Auto.

RightBlogger's automation caps at 30 queued posts and 5 per site per day. DispatchSEO's counts are flat: a Scale site gets five a week, and a thin niche may get fewer because the research step never queues a topic without measured volume and difficulty.

## How each one publishes to a repo

RightBlogger's Git integration ("Continue with GitHub", pick a repo, or paste a URL and token) finds where your posts live, commits each post with its images, and opens a pull request by default. A toggle commits straight to the production branch instead. It supports GitHub and Cloudflare Artifacts and any Markdown or MDX blog (Astro, Next.js, Hugo, Eleventy, Jekyll). It verifies that the change reached the live site and asks the host to rebuild if not.

DispatchSEO's server does the same mechanics: it reads a post already in your repo and mirrors its frontmatter keys, date format and file naming, commits the article on a branch, opens the pull request, and merges it when the site is on Auto. A verify job then confirms the page is live and rebuilds a red pull request twice before leaving a comment. It also supports a hosted blog when the site has no repo, and self-hosted WordPress through its API.

Where RightBlogger is ahead: Cloudflare Artifacts, and a mature product around the integration. Where DispatchSEO is ahead: the merge step is automatic, and the article's Google clicks and impressions appear in the same dashboard a week later.

## What is in the article

RightBlogger's pricing page does not mention fact-checking or source citations in generated posts; its Site Agent proposes internal-link and SEO edits after publishing. DispatchSEO's pipeline filters facts for usefulness and faithfulness to their quoted source, assigns facts to sections, checks every claim against the cited passage, measures overlap with page-one competitors so the article is not a rewrite of what already ranks, and parks the topic if it cannot be grounded in more than one source. Each article ships with internal links to your existing pages and a drawn cover image.

## Who should not pick DispatchSEO

Anyone on Wix, Squarespace, Shopify or Webflow. Anyone who wants 30 posts a month. Anyone who also wants a writing assistant for their own drafts; DispatchSEO has none. Anyone who manages ten client sites on one bill; RightBlogger's Agency plan is built for exactly that.

## Who should not pick RightBlogger

Anyone who wants to try before paying: the free tier writes 2,000 words and does not automate. Anyone whose site has no repository and no supported CMS; RightBlogger does not host a blog. Anyone who wants a fact check in the pipeline rather than an audit afterwards.
