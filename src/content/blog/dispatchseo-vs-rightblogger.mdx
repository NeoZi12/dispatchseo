Pick RightBlogger if you also write your own posts, want about 30 automated posts a month on one site, or publish to Webflow, Ghost or Duda. Pick DispatchSEO if you want to see real published articles before paying, your site has no repo and needs a hosted blog, or you want a fact check inside the pipeline rather than an audit afterwards. Those are the two tools that will commit an article to a GitHub repository and open a pull request; past that they are built for different people. We make DispatchSEO, so RightBlogger's side is quoted from its own pages.

> **TL;DR** - RightBlogger: a writing suite with automation, $49 a month billed yearly ($59 monthly) for one site and one automated post a day; the free tier writes 2,000 words and does not automate. DispatchSEO: a narrower autopilot that publishes two fact-checked articles a month free (with a small badge) and one to five a week for $19 to $69. Both open pull requests; only DispatchSEO merges them for you when the site is on Auto, and only DispatchSEO can host the blog when there is no repo.

RightBlogger caps automation at 30 queued posts and 5 per site per day. DispatchSEO's counts are flat, and a thin niche may get fewer because the research step never queues a topic without measured volume and difficulty.

## How does each one publish to a repo?

RightBlogger's Git integration ("Continue with GitHub", or paste a repo URL and token) finds where your posts live, commits each post with its images, and opens a pull request by default. A toggle commits to the production branch instead. It supports GitHub and Cloudflare Artifacts and any Markdown or MDX blog (Astro, Next.js, Hugo, Eleventy, Jekyll), verifies the change reached the live site, and asks the host to rebuild if not.

DispatchSEO's server does the same mechanics: it reads a post already in your repo and mirrors its frontmatter keys, date format and file naming, commits on a branch, opens the pull request, and merges it when the site is on Auto. A verify job confirms the page is live and rebuilds a red pull request twice before leaving a comment. When there is no repo, it hosts the blog, and it publishes to self-hosted WordPress through the WordPress API.

RightBlogger is ahead on Cloudflare Artifacts and on the product built around the integration. DispatchSEO is ahead on the automatic merge and on showing each article's Google clicks in the same dashboard a week later.

## What is in the article?

RightBlogger's pricing page does not mention fact-checking or source citations in generated posts; its Site Agent proposes internal-link and SEO edits after publishing. DispatchSEO's pipeline keeps only facts it can tie to a quoted source, checks every claim against the passage it cites, measures overlap with the pages already on page one so the article is not a rewrite of them, and parks the topic if it cannot be grounded in more than one source. Each article ships with internal links to your existing pages and a cover image.

## Who should pick which?

RightBlogger is the better fit for anyone on Wix, Squarespace, Shopify or Webflow (DispatchSEO does not support them), for anyone who wants 30 posts a month, for anyone who wants a writing assistant for their own drafts, and for an agency running ten client sites on one bill. DispatchSEO is the better fit for anyone who wants to try before paying, whose site has no repository and no supported CMS, or who wants the fact check before publication rather than an audit after it.

## FAQ

**Can I use both?** Yes, on different sites. On the same repo they would each open pull requests and the merge order would be yours to manage.

**Which one is cheaper for one site at one post a week?** DispatchSEO Starter at $19 a month. RightBlogger has no plan below $49 a month billed yearly.
