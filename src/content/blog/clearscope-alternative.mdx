## What Clearscope actually does now: five modules, not one score

<ClearscopeModuleGrid />

Clearscope's own pricing page, read live while writing this: Essentials runs $129/mo for 50 tracked prompts, 50 pages, 20 topic explorations, and 20 AI-assisted drafts a month; Business is $399/mo with 300 prompts and pages plus a dedicated account manager; Enterprise is custom, adding SSO and crawler whitelisting. "Tracked prompts" is the tell - that's Expand, the module that checks whether AI answer engines cite your brand for a given query, sitting next to Write's AI drafting and Optimize's term recommendations. None of the five modules publishes anything. Clearscope's own product page says so directly: you still take the draft, the brief, or the citation gap it surfaces and act on it yourself, through your CMS or WordPress integration.

MarketMuse is the one holdout: its pricing page shows nothing about AI-citation tracking, only topic research and content briefs.

## The step none of them replace: the draft, the review, the ship

## DispatchSEO's own Discover-to-Protect loop, minus the editor

DispatchSEO runs the same job Clearscope splits into five modules, just without a screen for any of them. Discover and Optimize happen during keyword research, against this project's own DataForSEO account rather than Clearscope's index. Write is the agent drafting the full page - prose, bespoke visuals, and a cover - not a Google Doc with AI suggestions in the margin. Ship is the part none of the five modules do: the draft opens as a pull request, reviewed like any other change to the repo, not exported or auto-published around a review step. And Expand and Protect run after that, on the same schedule as everything else - Search Console-tracked rank plus [this project's own AI-citation tracking](/blog/ai-visibility-tracking-tool), the numbers above pulled live from `get_ai_visibility` rather than asserted. Zero citations 21 days in isn't a flattering number, but it's the same cold-start any new site has, tracked by a $129/mo module or not.

## Where a team editor still wins over one repo and one agent

None of this makes Clearscope or its four look-alikes pointless. If a team of writers is going to draft pages by hand regardless, and what you actually want is a shared editor where everyone sees the same term-coverage score and citation-tracking dashboard update as they work, that's a real, different job - the one all five of these products are built and priced for, dedicated account managers included at the top tier. DispatchSEO has no equivalent screen for a second person to open. There's no live score, no shared brief, no seat to invite a co-writer into - the review surface is a pull request diff, and after that, a rank and citation check on a schedule.

## Honest scope: no shared editor, no team seats, no live score

Stay on Clearscope, NeuronWriter, Frase, or Surfer if a human writer - you, a hire, a freelancer - is doing the drafting either way and the tool's job is to tell that writer whether the draft is competitive before it goes out, or if multiple people need to collaborate on the same brief at once. If the actual question is narrower than any of that - whether a page you're about to write already overlaps one you published last year - [a free keyword cannibalization checker](/free-tools/keyword-cannibalization-checker) answers it without a subscription to any of the five. Move to a research-to-PR pipeline if the part you don't want to do is the drafting itself, not just the scoring - deciding what to cover, writing it, and shipping it as a change instead of a document someone still has to publish by hand. If it's a full keyword-and-backlink suite you're weighing instead of a content-scoring editor, [that's a different comparison](/blog/ahrefs-alternative) - adjacent category, same "does it actually do the work" question.

## FAQ

**Is Clearscope still just a term-coverage scoring tool?**
No - checked live on its own pricing page, Clearscope now ships five modules: Discover (topic research), Write (AI drafting), Optimize (term scoring), Expand (AI-citation tracking), and Protect (published-page monitoring). It still doesn't publish anything itself; every module hands you something to act on.

**Do Clearscope's competitors track AI-citations too?**
Most do. NeuronWriter and Frase both track citations across ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews, and (on higher tiers) Perplexity, Claude, and Gemini. Surfer has an AI Tracker feature live now, plus an autonomous "Surfy Agent" it has announced but not shipped. MarketMuse is the exception - its current pricing page shows no AI-citation tracking at all.

**Does anything on this list ship the page itself?**
Only partially. Frase's Scale tier ($239-299/mo) can auto-publish drafted content straight to a CMS, but with no review step or pull request in between. Nothing else on the shortlist publishes at all - Clearscope, NeuronWriter, MarketMuse, and Surfer (pending its unshipped agent) all stop at the draft or the score.

**What does DispatchSEO replace if I'm currently paying for Clearscope?**
The research, drafting, and publishing loop - Clearscope's Discover, Write, and the acting-on-Optimize's-score step, plus getting the page live. It doesn't replace a shared editor or a live-updating dashboard for a team; there isn't one here to open.

**Is DispatchSEO's AI-citation tracking the same as Clearscope's Expand module?**
Same job, different shape - Expand is a dashboard you check; `get_ai_visibility` returns the same kind of numbers (queries checked, citation rate, per-engine breakdown) as MCP state, checked live in this piece rather than screenshotted from a UI.

Every name on today's page 1 for "clearscope alternative" - the five-module version of Clearscope included - is still answering "how do I know if my draft is good, and am I [getting cited](/blog/how-to-rank-in-chatgpt)." That's a real product category, and worth paying for if a person is writing the draft regardless. It's just not the same question as "who does the drafting and the shipping," and nothing on today's page 1 names the option that skips straight to a reviewed, tracked page.
