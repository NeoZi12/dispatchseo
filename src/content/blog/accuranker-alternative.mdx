## What AccuRanker actually tracks, and how fast it updates

AccuRanker's homepage claims are specific about scope, not about numbers: "real-time updates" with "insights in just seconds," a company "trusted by thousands of SEO professionals worldwide since 2013," and over 50 filters for organizing a tracked keyword list. The product itself is one job, done several ways - keyword rank tracking, a keyword research database, competitor tracking at the group/URL/product level, forecasting, and customizable reporting dashboards. AI-powered CTR and search-volume metrics, Google Data Studio integration, and unlimited read-API access arrive at the Expert tier; raw SERP HTML and BigQuery access are Enterprise-only. None of that list includes a content step. AccuRanker doesn't draft, queue, or publish anything - it's a precision instrument for the one question "where do I rank right now," answered fast and answered often.

## Who still needs AccuRanker, and who just needs the loop

Stay on AccuRanker (or a similarly dedicated tracker) when the job genuinely is scale and precision - an agency watching thousands of keywords across a dozen client domains, needing BigQuery exports, a dedicated customer success manager, or SERP HTML at the raw-data level no self-hosted setup replicates. It's also the right pick for anyone who wants a chart and nothing more: some teams have a person whose job is reading rank dashboards and deciding what to do next, and AccuRanker is built precisely for that person. Where the job is narrower - one site, rankings and Search Console watched together, and [a content pipeline that turns a gap into a shipped page](/blog/outrank-alternative) without a human doing the research-to-PR handoff by hand - tracking 45 keywords on AccuRanker's cheapest tier would mean paying for 1,955 keywords' worth of headroom nobody's using.

## FAQ

**How much does AccuRanker cost?**
Three tiers verified on AccuRanker's own pricing page: Professional at $224/month for 2,000-5,000 keywords, Expert at $764/month for 10,000-25,000 keywords, and custom Enterprise pricing above 25,000 keywords, with a 10% discount for annual billing.

**Does AccuRanker do anything besides rank tracking?**
It adds keyword research, competitor tracking, and reporting dashboards around the core rank-tracking job, plus AI-powered CTR and search-volume metrics at the Expert tier. None of that extends into drafting or publishing content - every module ends at a report a person reads.

**Does DispatchSEO track keywords as fast as AccuRanker?**
No, and this guide doesn't pretend otherwise. AccuRanker advertises real-time updates; this project's own tracker checks each keyword once a night, on the same cron that also pulls Search Console. The trade is what happens after the check, not the speed of the check itself.

**Can DispatchSEO track thousands of keywords like an agency would need?**
Nothing here is built for that scale. This project tracks 45 keywords total, sized to one site's own content queue, not a multi-client agency book - AccuRanker's Expert and Enterprise tiers exist specifically for that job.

**What happens when a tracked keyword's position changes here?**
It's checked against Search Console data for the same query, then against a live SERP for whether a new or refreshed page could beat what's currently ranking - only if it can does it become a suggestion queued for a build, the same [rank-tracking-to-content loop](/blog/moz-alternative) this project runs for every tracked keyword, not just this one.

**Is there a cheaper way to try rank tracking before running a self-hosted setup?**
DataForSEO's own SERP API costs a fraction of a cent per check and can be called directly with no dashboard at all - what that actually costs in practice, and what building a tracker on top of it takes, is a separate build-vs-buy question from this one.

AccuRanker earns its price at the scale it's built for - thousands of keywords, an agency's worth of client domains, a person whose whole job is reading the dashboard it produces. Most people typing "AccuRanker alternative" for one site are paying for headroom they'll never use to answer a question that was never the hard part: not where you rank, but what happens the moment you find out.
