A solo founder's SaaS marketing strategy comes down to one sorting rule: put your first hours into channels that keep paying after you stop (SEO pages, docs, free tools, product-led loops) and treat everything that resets to zero each week (ads, cold outreach, social posts) as a supplement. Use launch channels for your first users while SEO warms up, and add paid only once you know which keywords convert.

> **TL;DR** - Sort every channel as "compounds" or "resets". Months 1-3: launch in communities for first users and start SEO the same week. Months 4-9: one topic, steady pages, read Search Console. Months 10-12: test paid on keywords that already convert. Expect slow clicks from search - this site's first 28 days produced 10.

## Which SaaS marketing channels keep paying after you stop?

The ones where the work is a durable asset. A ranking page, a docs article or a free tool is found by new people next month with no further effort from you. A paid ad, a cold email or a social post is found by nobody the moment you stop.

That split matters more when you are alone than in a team, because your constraint is hours, not budget. A team can run five resetting channels in parallel. You can run one or two, and every hour spent on a channel that resets is an hour you rebuy next week.

Resetting channels are not bad. They are fast. Launch days, communities and outreach are how you get your first users while a young site has no search visibility at all. The mistake is making them the whole plan.

## What should you run in months 1 to 3 to get the first users?

A small number of launch moves, aimed at the places your buyers already talk. Pick two, not six.

- **One or two communities** where your users ask questions. Answer them properly and mention the product only where it is the real answer.
- **One launch day** on a directory or forum that fits your product. Expect a spike and then the baseline, so do it when the product can hold a stranger's attention.
- **Direct conversations** with the first dozen users. Their wording becomes your keyword list later.

None of this compounds, so keep it time-boxed. Give it a few hours a week and protect the rest for the channel that does.

## Why does SEO start in week one if it pays in month six?

Because the delay is the whole reason to start early. Search has a waiting period that no amount of effort removes: Google has to find, index and begin to trust a new domain. Starting in month six means the clock starts in month six.

This site is a clean example of how slow it is. Over the last 28 days dispatchseo.com logged 284 impressions and 10 clicks, read with the site's own Search Console stats while writing this guide.

Seven of those clicks are attributed to people searching for the brand name. None are attributed to a non-brand query, and the comparison pages that earned impressions sat around positions 40 to 90. That is a young domain's honest picture: Google shows you for your own name quickly and makes you earn everything else.

Two lessons for the strategy. First, brand searches are a lagging result of other channels, such as a launch or a community mention, so do not read them as proof SEO works. Second, the non-brand impressions are the early signal that matters, because they tell you which topic Google is starting to test you on. ## When does paid acquisition start to make sense?

After you know which keywords convert, not before. Paid search charges you for every click, so buying traffic for a keyword you have never seen turn into a signup is paying to run an experiment.

The order that wastes least:

1. Rank a page organically for a commercial keyword and watch whether its visitors sign up.
2. If they do, you have a conversion rate and a landing page that works.
3. Only then bid on that keyword, or its close variants, to take more of the same traffic.

Paid is a multiplier for something you have already proven, not a way to discover it. If you cannot yet say what a signup is worth, you cannot yet say what a click is worth.

## What does one week of marketing look like?

Small and repeatable. A weekly rhythm beats an occasional sprint because compounding channels reward consistency.

| Task | Time | Channel type |
|---|---|---|
| Research one keyword and write or publish one page | the bulk of the week | Compounds |
| Read Search Console: queries by impressions, indexed pages | about 20 minutes | Compounds |
| Answer questions in one community | 1-2 hours | Resets |
| Talk to one user or prospect | 1 hour | Feeds the keyword list |

These time figures are my planning assumptions, not measurements. Adjust them to your week, but keep the shape: most of the hours go to the channel that compounds.

The expectations are deliberately modest, because the data above is modest. In the first quarter, expect early users from launches and communities, and an indexed site with almost no search clicks. By the middle of the year, expect impressions to grow and a handful of clicks, mostly from your own name. First real non-brand clicks tend to arrive on pages that reached page 1 or 2, which is why the plan feeds the topic Search Console shows rather than spreading across ten.

Treat this as a planning shape, not a forecast. A site with existing links or an audience will move faster, and a market with little search demand will move slower.

## When is this strategy wrong for you?

If you need revenue in the next 30 days, search is the wrong first channel: its first clicks take weeks to months, and this site's own data shows it. If your product has no search demand, such as a new category nobody names yet, lean harder on communities and direct outreach. If you sell to a handful of large accounts, outbound conversations will beat content for a long time. And if you already have an audience or a lot of links, skip the slow start and aim at competitive keywords sooner.

## FAQ

**What is the best SaaS marketing strategy for a solo founder?**
Run one or two launch channels for your first users while you start SEO and content the same week. Search is slow but keeps paying, and launches and outreach do not.

**Which marketing channels compound?**
SEO pages, documentation, free tools and product-led loops such as shared outputs. They keep bringing in visitors after the work is done.

**When should a SaaS start paid ads?**
After you know which keywords convert. Organic pages show you which queries sign up, and paid then buys more of that proven traffic.

**How long does SaaS SEO take to work?**
Expect impressions in weeks and meaningful non-brand clicks over months. This site logged 10 clicks in its latest 28 days, mostly from brand searches.

**Can one person run all of this?**
Yes, if you pick a small number of channels and keep a weekly rhythm. Automating research, drafting and tracking frees your hours for users and decisions.
